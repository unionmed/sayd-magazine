George Taza: We Must All Take Part in Protecting Fish Stocks

12 November 2022Sayd

اقرأ بالعربية

The sea has a world of its own! Underwater we enjoy watching marine creatures, coral, and caves; on the surface we enjoy the calm of the horizon, we rest under the sun, we answer the moonlight, and we dance to the sound of its waves.

That is how George Taza, administrator of the “Lebanese fishermen” page, sees it.

From the conversation:

  • I began this hobby on my first outing with my father in 1999.
  • Fishers face many problems, including illegal poaching: putting poison in bread and throwing it to catch fish, blasting, and bottom-dredging — all violations with grave consequences for fish stocks.
  • Fishers fail to respect marine fishing laws because there is no accountability, no monitoring, and too few awareness programmes from the ministry concerned.
  • The state as a whole is responsible for monitoring and for stopping the dumping of waste into the sea, because the sea is a livelihood for many fishers.
  • Fishing is both a hobby and work, and it always needs to evolve. Every fisher’s experience helps raise the level of this hobby.
  • “The sea is treacherous”… but in our lexicon it is fishing and passion. That does not cancel the need for caution, for the sake of safety.
  • The landfill project over the seal cave in Amchit was stopped by a decision of the Ministry of Environment after complaints from environmental activists, and after we moved quickly and published photographs on social media.
  • Women compete with men in sea fishing: they have patience and a long fuse.
  • It often happens, when you haul the line, that a large fish comes to prey on the fish already hooked, and pulls the line packed with hooks; the fish, defending its life, can also turn on you. That is one of the risks of fishing.
  • We laugh a great deal when a fish slips off the hook inside the boat, between our legs — and also when fish jump at night as the boat is underway and come in on us.
  • The sea’s gifts are many. We must all take part in protecting fish stocks and respecting the law.
  • What is needed: awareness in schools, follow-up in fish markets, awareness programmes in the media, more visits to harbours, and attention to fishers’ problems so they are brought into the work of protecting fish stocks.

Lynn Araji: Equestrian Champion and Mental-Arithmetic Champion

22 October 2022Sayd

اقرأ بالعربية

Equestrianism does not submit to the will of age, but to the will to love a challenge and to a strong will. That is why it is a kind of private arithmetic, with equations of its own, that hits its targets in a leap of light.

Lynn Araji is an equestrian champion with a Lebanese signature — and a mental-arithmetic champion with a worldwide one.

Sayd sat with her for this conversation. She said:

  • I first met horses at the age of ten, trained by my cousin Sheikh Imran Araji, who was hard on me so that I would give my best.
  • The Araji family has been known since old times for its love of horses and for keeping them.
  • The horse gives you confidence and strengthens your character.
  • My first jump was in a Beirut competition. I did not feel fear, because I was determined not to be afraid. The feeling was beautiful, and I sensed that I had to clear every fence the same way.
  • We have to calculate every step — in a horse jump or in a jump in life. That idea strengthened me in mental arithmetic and in my win at Genius Map: I placed third in the Bekaa and fourth in the world. I also want to become a world champion in equestrian sport and to compete outside Lebanon.
  • The mare feels the rider’s fear, and that is why she may turn on him and throw him. A rider should try several horses, to understand their mistakes more clearly and to correct them through experience.
  • Of course I would like to have a farm.
  • I know enough about the world of horses, but I do not like training them.
  • In training we jump at a height of 100 cm; in competitions, between 60 and 90 cm.
  • To date I have taken part in nine competitions and won seven of them.
  • I do not have a horse of my own. I love Duha because she was the first mare I trained on, and I still do. If I had my own horse I would name it Reem.
  • Away from horses, I swim and I box.
  • If I marry one day, I will want my husband to love horses too.
  • I could never eat horse meat — quite simply because I do not eat meat.
  • I would like to compete in every Arab country, but I lean toward competing in Jordan.
  • I have fallen off more than once. Once I broke my arm and stopped training for four months. Nothing stops me from going on, as the saying goes: if we do not fall, we do not learn.
  • I love both: arithmetic and horses. A rider’s heart loves everything.

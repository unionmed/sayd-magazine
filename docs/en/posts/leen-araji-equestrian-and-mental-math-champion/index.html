Leen Araji: Equestrian Champion and Mental Math Champion

22 October 2022Sayd

Equestrian sport answers not to age but to the will to challenge. Sayd meets Leen Araji — a Lebanese jumping champion and a world-ranked mental math talent.

Equestrian sport is not subject to the will of age, but to the will to challenge and to strong resolve.

That is why it is a kind of special arithmetic, with special equations, that reaches its goals in a leap of light.

Leen Araji is an equestrian champion with a Lebanese signature — and a mental math champion with a global one.

Sayd held this conversation with her.

She said in the interview:

  • I was introduced to horses at the age of 10, trained by my cousin Sheikh Omran Araji, who was hard on me so I would achieve the best performance.
  • The Araji family has long been known for loving and keeping horses.
  • Horses give confidence and strengthen personal force.
  • My first jump was in a Beirut competition. I felt no fear because I was determined not to fear; the feeling was beautiful, and I sensed I had to clear every fence the same way.
  • We must calculate every step — in a horse jump or in a leap of life. That idea strengthened my mental math and my win in “Genius Map”: I ranked third in the Beqaa and fourth worldwide. I also want to become a world equestrian champion and to compete outside Lebanon.
  • A horse feels the rider’s fear, and for that reason may turn against them and throw them. A rider should try several horses to understand their mistakes better and correct them through experience.
  • Of course I would love to have a farm.
  • I know enough about the horse world, but I do not enjoy training horses myself.
  • In training we jump at heights of 100 cm; in competitions, between 60 and 90 cm.
  • To date I have entered nine competitions and won seven of them.
  • I do not have my own horse. I love “Doha” because she was the first mare I trained on and still do; if I had my own horse I would name him “Reem.”
  • Outside equestrian sport my hobbies are swimming and boxing.
  • If I marry one day, I will make my husband love horses.
  • I could never eat horse meat — quite simply, I do not eat meat.
  • I love competing in all Arab countries, but I lean toward Jordan.
  • I have fallen from the horse more than once; once I broke my arm and stopped training for four months — but nothing keeps me from continuing, as the saying goes: “If we don’t fall, we don’t learn.”
  • I love both: math and horses. A rider’s heart loves everything.

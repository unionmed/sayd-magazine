Bird Encyclopedia

Hen Harrier

6 November 2026Sayd
Share this story

Circus cyaneus

Identity and identification

The Hen Harrier is an Accipitridae raptor with long wings and tail. Adult males have a grey head and breast, pale belly and extensive black wingtips. Females and juveniles are brown with a white rump, but that feature alone does not identify every harrier.

Habitat and food

It quarters fields, open ground and wetlands in low flight for small mammals and birds. Wing shape, plumage and flight style together help separate it from Pallid and Montagu’s Harriers.

Breeding and development

BTO reports a typical clutch of 4–5 eggs and female incubation of about 34 days. Young depend on parental care and normally fledge after 36.5–41.5 days. These general stages do not demonstrate breeding in a passage or wintering country.

Arab occurrence and seasonality

Lebanon’s 2026 guide describes an uncommon passage migrant and winter visitor. Oman’s official list, version 8.0 dated 1 January 2026, gives the same broad status. November belongs to the passage and wintering context; a sighting does not establish local breeding.

Observation and conservation

Watch raptors from a distance without pursuing them or approaching communal roosts. Reducing poisoning and electrocution and retaining open habitat supports their conservation.

<figure><img src="../../../media/publications/daily-birds-20261110/oxyura-leucocephala.jpg" width="2048" height="1229" alt="Male White-headed Duck with a swollen blue bill swimming on water" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style="display:block;width:100%;height:auto"><figcaption>White-headed Duck</figcaption></figure><p class="excerpt">Oxyura leucocephala</p><h2>Identity, life and distribution</h2><p>White-headed Duck Oxyura leucocephala is a compact diving duck with a stiff tail often held raised. Adult males have a largely white head, dark crown and blue bill in breeding condition. Females have browner heads with contrasting facial stripes. Male colours should not be applied to all ages or seasons.</p><p>It uses lakes and wetlands with reeds and submerged vegetation, diving for food that includes important aquatic-invertebrate resources. Nests and ducklings are vulnerable to drying and vegetation changes during the breeding season.</p><p>A cupped nest is built over water among emergent stems and leaves. A clutch contains about 4–9 eggs, incubated for 22–24 days; the female then tends the young, which fledge after about 8–10 weeks. The bird measures roughly 43–48 cm, and stable water and vegetation remain important throughout this cycle.</p><p>Local breeding populations occur in Arab North Africa. A study published in 2026 documented successful breeding observed in western Algeria in 2020. Nesting was also documented at Dar Bouazza, Morocco, in 2025. Such sites are particularly important for maintaining local populations.</p><p>Habitat loss, drought and pollution keep it a conservation priority. Hybridisation with introduced Ruddy Ducks adds a threat to the integrity of its populations. Conservation programmes therefore address both wetland restoration and this hybridisation risk.</p><p class="photo-credit">Photo: karim Attouche</p>

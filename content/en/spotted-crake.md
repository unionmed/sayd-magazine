<figure><img src="../../../media/publications/daily-birds-20261106/spotted-crake.jpg" width="1740" height="1308" alt="Spotted Crake with a short yellow bill and spotted plumage among reeds" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style="display:block;width:100%;height:auto"><figcaption>Spotted Crake</figcaption></figure><p class="excerpt">Porzana porzana</p><h2>Identity and identification</h2><p>The Spotted Crake is a small, compact Rallidae species with a short yellowish bill tinged red at the base and greenish legs. White spotting marks the head and body, with barring on the flanks. Its short bill contrasts with the long bill of Water Rail.</p><h2>Habitat and food</h2><p>It uses shallow marshes and vegetated muddy margins, feeding on invertebrates and plant material. It often stays concealed, rewarding patient observation at a habitat edge.</p><h2>Breeding and development</h2><p>BTO reports a typical clutch of 10–12 eggs and shared incubation lasting 18–19 days. Downy chicks can move about early, but flight normally develops at 43–56 days. Leaving the nest site therefore differs from becoming capable of flight.</p><h2>Arab occurrence and seasonality</h2><p>Lebanon’s 2026 guide describes common spring passage, scarce autumn passage and rare winter presence from mid-November to late February. Qatar Bird Observation Group lists scarce passage. Winter status should therefore not be transferred from one country to the other.</p><h2>Observation and conservation</h2><p>The species is globally listed as Least Concern (LC) on the IUCN Red List; local habitat protection remains important. The Lebanon guide lists wetland drainage and destruction, together with agricultural expansion, among the main threats. Retaining marsh vegetation and shallow water preserves resting and feeding habitat. Watch without entering reeds or driving birds out for a photograph.</p><h2>References</h2><p>AviList Core Team — Global Avian Checklist v2026, 20 October 2026؛ UNDP / ABCL / Ministry of Environment — Birds of Lebanon: A Photographic Guide to 409 Species, 18 June 2026؛ Qatar Bird Observation Group — Qatar Bird List, consulted 6 November 2026؛ British Trust for Ornithology — BirdFacts: Spotted Crake؛ RSPB — Spotted Crake</p><p class="photo-credit">Species illustration: Лариса Артемьева — CC0 1.0.</p>

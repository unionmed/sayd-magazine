<figure><img src="../../../media/publications/daily-birds-20261105/water-pipit.jpg" width="1211" height="1211" alt="Water Pipit holding insects while perched on a branch" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style="display:block;width:100%;height:auto"><figcaption>Water Pipit</figcaption></figure><p class="excerpt">Anthus spinoletta</p><h2>Identity and identification</h2><p>Water Pipit Anthus spinoletta belongs to Motacillidae. Winter plumage shows a pale eyebrow, streaked breast and usually dark legs. The breeding breast becomes less streaked and pinkish. Compare face, wings and legs with Meadow Pipit: a photograph beside water cannot establish identity by itself.</p><h2>Habitat and food</h2><p>It collects insects and other invertebrates from damp ground and water margins. Populations breed in mountain grasslands, moving to lower, warmer habitats outside breeding.</p><h2>Breeding and development</h2><p>The ground nest is concealed among vegetation. BTO gives a typical clutch of 4–5 eggs, female incubation of 14–15 days and nest departure around 15–16 days. Hatchlings cannot support themselves and need feeding; nest departure is not full independence.</p><h2>Arab occurrence and seasonality</h2><p>Lebanon’s 2020 checklist describes widespread passage from mid-September to late November and mid-February to early May, with scarce, local wintering in wetlands. Qatar’s current list classifies it as a common winter visitor. November thus connects Lebanese passage with Qatar’s winter season, at different abundance.</p><h2>Observation and conservation</h2><p>Retaining wet margins and damp grassland preserves feeding habitat. Watch without repeatedly displacing birds between feeding patches.</p><p class="article-sources">British Trust for Ornithology — BirdFacts: Water Pipit · RSPB — Water Pipit · Ramadan-Jaradi et al. — Updated checklist of the birds of Lebanon, Sandgrouse 42 (2020) · Qatar Bird Observation Group — Qatar Bird List · AviList Core Team — Global Avian Checklist v2026, 20 October 2026</p><p class="photo-credit">Species illustration: Paulo Caseirito — CC0 1.0. · iNaturalist: 219774448</p>

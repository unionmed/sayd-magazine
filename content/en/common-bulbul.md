<figure><img src="../../../media/publications/birds-catchup-20261104-d/pycnonotus-barbatus.jpg" width="1461" height="1546" alt="Common Bulbul — Pycnonotus barbatus" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style="display:block;width:100%;height:auto"><figcaption>Common Bulbul</figcaption></figure><p class="excerpt">Pycnonotus barbatus</p><h2>Identity and identification</h2><p>Common Bulbul Pycnonotus barbatus belongs to Pycnonotidae. Maghreb birds have a dark head, grey-brown body and pale undertail, without a large white cheek or striking white eye-ring. Colour varies across its African range, so southern photographs should not represent every geographical population.</p><h2>Habitat, food and behaviour</h2><p>Gardens, orchards, woodland and cultivated edges provide habitat. It eats fruit, insects and other plant material, appearing in pairs or small groups. Algerian diet studies document fruit and invertebrates and links with seasonally changing resources.</p><h2>Breeding and parental care</h2><p>A cup nest is placed in dense vegetation or a suitable tree. Young need provisioning before and after nest departure. Breeding timing varies with region and conditions. Rarely seeing concealed nests does not imply that local birds fail to breed.</p><h2>Range and Arab occurrence</h2><p>Widespread in Africa, it is familiar as a resident in parts of Morocco, Algeria and Tunisia. It should not be equated with White-spectacled Bulbul in the Levant or White-eared Bulbul in Iraq and the Gulf: these are separate species with different marks and ranges.</p><h2>Seasonality and observation</h2><p>November observation is possible in resident Maghreb areas, particularly at fruiting trees. Record undertail colour, head pattern and photograph location. A vernacular record naming only a bulbul cannot establish species without description or a clear image.</p><h2>Ecological role and conservation</h2><p>It disperses some fruit seeds and consumes insects. Scrub clearance, pesticides and bird trade are pressures. Diverse gardens with native fruiting plants preserve food and refuge; concealed nests should remain covered rather than exposed for photography.</p><h2>References</h2><p>Revue d’Écologie (La Terre et la Vie) 60 (2005) — food ecology of Pycnonotus barbatus in Algeria.</p><p>Milla et al. — Régime alimentaire du Bulbul des jardins dans le Sahel algérois, Revue d’Écologie 60 (2005), 369–380.</p><p class="photo-credit">Photo: karim Attouche — CC0 1.0.</p>

The crest may be raised or flattened, so its height in a single photograph is not decisive.</p><p>It is associated with European woodland, especially coniferous habitats. It searches branches and trunks for insects and pine seeds, stores food for winter and may join mixed tit flocks.</p><p>The female excavates a nest site in suitable dead wood and lines it with soft material. BTO gives a typical clutch of five or six eggs. Suitable dead wood is therefore part of a living forest’s nesting infrastructure.</p><p>The photograph was taken in Estonia and illustrates the species, not its occurrence in Morocco.</p><p>Avibase, using Clements treatment, mentions rare vagrancy to Morocco. That differs from an established population.</p><p class="article-sources"><a href="https://www.bto.org/learn/about-birds/birdfacts/crested-tit">www.bto.org — Crested Tit</a> · <a href="https://www.rspb.org.uk/birds-and-wildlife/crested-tit">www.rspb.org.uk — Crested Tit</a> · <a href="https://avibase.bsc-eoc.org/species.jsp?avibaseid=E553193354C74B21&amp;lang=EN">avibase.bsc-eoc.org — Crested Tit</a></p><p class="photo-credit">Photo: Yehor Yatsiuk</p>

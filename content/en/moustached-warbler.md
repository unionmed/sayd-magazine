<figure><img src="../../../media/publications/daily-birds-20261106/moustached-warbler.jpg" width="985" height="746" alt="Moustached Warbler with a broad pale eyebrow moving among reed stems" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style="display:block;width:100%;height:auto"><figcaption>Moustached Warbler</figcaption></figure><p class="excerpt">Acrocephalus melanopogon</p><h2>Identity and identification</h2><p>The Moustached Warbler is an Acrocephalidae species with a broad pale eyebrow contrasting with a dark crown and darker cheek. It has streaked brown upperparts and pale underparts with brownish flanks; a clear view of the head helps separate similar reedbed warblers.</p><h2>Habitat and food</h2><p>It searches aquatic vegetation and reeds for small invertebrates, including beetles and tiny snails. Dense wetland vegetation is central to its habitat, making drainage and reed burning important pressures.</p><h2>Breeding and development</h2><p>SEO/BirdLife describes a cup nest within aquatic vegetation and a clutch of 3–6 eggs. Both parents incubate for about 14–15 days and feed the chicks, which become capable of flight at around 12 days. Breeding dates vary between regions.</p><h2>Arab occurrence and seasonality</h2><p>The 2026 Lebanon guide lists it as a common summer breeder, scarce passage migrant and rare winter visitor. Its breeding abundance does not imply similar winter numbers; this account does not give precise dates for autumn passage.</p><h2>Observation and conservation</h2><p>The species is globally listed as Least Concern (LC) on the IUCN Red List; local habitat protection remains important. The Lebanon guide also lists pesticides and illegal hunting among the pressures affecting the species. Wait for it at a vegetation edge rather than forcing a route through reeds. Failure to detect this secretive bird during a short visit does not establish absence.</p><h2>References</h2><p>AviList Core Team — Global Avian Checklist v2026, 20 October 2026؛ UNDP / ABCL / Ministry of Environment — Birds of Lebanon: A Photographic Guide to 409 Species, 18 June 2026؛ SEO/BirdLife — Carricerín real؛ BirdLife International — Acrocephalus melanopogon, The IUCN Red List of Threatened Species, 2018</p><p class="photo-credit">Species illustration: Лариса Артемьева — CC0 1.0.</p>

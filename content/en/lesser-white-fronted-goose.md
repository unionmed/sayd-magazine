Adults have a white forehead and dark belly markings. These may be incomplete in young birds. Separating it from Greater White-fronted Goose requires bill proportions, head shape and eye-ring, not apparent size alone.</p><p>It grazes vegetation in open habitats and uses water for resting and roosting. Populations breed in northern Eurasia and move south to wintering areas. Young leave the nest early and maintain family associations during parts of the annual cycle.</p><p>The nest is a ground scrape lined with vegetation and down in the northern breeding range. The female usually lays 4–6 eggs and incubates for about four weeks. Young leave the nest early, and the family moves between feeding and resting places; leaving the nest does not mean the young can already fly.</p><p>Iraq is an important wintering country. Studies describe local winter occurrence in the north and greater rarity in the southern marshes. Northern survey locations include Dukan and Mosul, within the network of sites used outside the breeding season.</p><p>AEWA reports global Vulnerable status, VU. Resemblance to more numerous geese complicates conservation. Protecting feeding and roosting sites, reducing disturbance and documenting sightings help track small and scattered populations.</p><p class="photo-credit">Photo: andrew2285</p>

<figure><img src="../../../media/publications/king-salman-birds-20261105/king-salman-vulture-2025.jpg" width="1600" height="1200" alt="A vulture in King Salman bin Abdulaziz Royal Reserve." loading="lazy" decoding="async" style="display:block;width:100%;height:auto"><figcaption>A vulture in King Salman bin Abdulaziz Royal Reserve, in an archive photo from 2025.</figcaption></figure><p class="excerpt">The reserve provides habitats for resident and migratory birds.</p><p>The King Salman bin Abdulaziz Royal Reserve Development Authority has announced that 296 bird species have been recorded within the reserve.</p><p>Species mentioned in the announcement include the griffon vulture, lappet-faced vulture, sooty falcon and Egyptian vulture.</p><p>Located in northern and northwestern Saudi Arabia, the reserve provides habitats where migratory birds can rest and feed, alongside resident species.</p><p>The authority compared the total with an earlier record of 190 species. In October 2024, it had announced the documentation of more than 290 bird species in the reserve.</p><p class="article-sources"><a href="https://www.spa.gov.sa/N2718039">Source: Saudi Press Agency, 4 November 2026.</a></p><p class="photo-credit">Photo: SPA / Saudi Photos Hub · CC BY-SA 4.0 · <a href="https://cc.spa.gov.sa/ar/news/2435253">1</a> · <a rel="license" href="https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/">https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/</a></p>

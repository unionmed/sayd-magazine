<figure><img src="../../../media/publications/mseilha-hyena-rescue-20261117/rescue-team.jpg" width="4000" height="1800" alt="Anti-Poaching Unit team and a veterinarian during the night-time hyena rescue in Mseilha" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style="display:block;width:100%;height:auto"></figure><p class="excerpt">A report from hunters in Ras Nhach led to a rescue coordinated with the Ministry of Agriculture and involving a veterinarian.</p><p>The Anti-Poaching Unit (APU) rescued a hyena caught in a trap intended for wild boar in Mseilha, North Lebanon, after receiving a call from hunters in Ras Nhach. The unit is part of the joint Lebanese–international operations structure of the Middle East Center for Sustainable Harvest and Anti-Poaching (MECSHAP) and CABS.</p><p>The operation was coordinated with the Ministry of Agriculture’s Rural Development and Livestock departments in North Lebanon. A ministry veterinarian took part alongside a hunter who is a member of the Civil Defence in the Batroun area, helping the team carry out the rescue and safeguard the animal.</p><p>After assessing the situation, the team sedated the hyena with an injection administered from a distance, allowing them to approach safely and free it from the trap. A veterinary examination found that its leg was not broken and that the injury was limited to a wound, which was treated in the field.</p><figure><img src="../../../media/publications/mseilha-hyena-rescue-20261117/veterinary-care.jpg" width="1200" height="1019" alt="Veterinarian and team members treating the hyena during the rescue" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style="display:block;width:100%;height:auto"></figure><p>The hyena was moved from the rugged wooded area to a more suitable location for monitoring. The team waited for the sedation to wear off and removed the adhesive tape used to secure its mouth and limbs during the rescue.</p><p>Once the animal had regained consciousness and its condition was confirmed to be stable, it was released back into its natural surroundings. The team stayed to monitor its movement until they were satisfied that it could walk normally and move off independently towards a safe area.</p><figure><img src="../../../media/publications/mseilha-hyena-rescue-20261117/hyena-walking.jpg" width="1198" height="852" alt="The hyena walking at the site at night after the rescue" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style="display:block;width:100%;height:auto"></figure><p>The operation formed part of cooperation with the Ministry of Agriculture under the tripartite memorandum of understanding and cooperation signed by the ministry, MECSHAP and IndyACT. The agreement aims to strengthen the protection of terrestrial and marine wildlife and support field interventions coordinated with the relevant authorities.</p><p>MECSHAP praised the Ras Nhach hunters for promptly reporting the trapped animal, as well as the Ministry of Agriculture’s cooperation and field response. The centre highlighted the role responsible hunters can play in protecting wildlife and helping rescue animals at risk.</p><figure><img src="../../../media/publications/mseilha-hyena-rescue-20261117/hyena-release.jpg" width="1200" height="943" alt="The hyena moving away in its natural surroundings after release" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style="display:block;width:100%;height:auto"></figure><p class="photo-credit">Photo source: MECSHAP – 16 November 2026.</p>

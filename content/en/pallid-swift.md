<figure><img src="../../../media/publications/birds-catchup-20261104-c/apus-pallidus.jpg" width="2048" height="1326" alt="Pallid Swift — Apus pallidus" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style="display:block;width:100%;height:auto"><figcaption>Pallid Swift</figcaption></figure><p class="excerpt">Apus pallidus</p><h2>Identity and identification</h2><p>Pallid Swift Apus pallidus belongs to Apodidae. Similar to Common Swift, it is often browner and paler, with a broader pale throat and contrast between wing coverts and flight feathers. The face may look paler. Light and age alter these subtle features; an overexposed photograph cannot identify it.</p><h2>Habitat, food and behaviour</h2><p>It catches insects and airborne invertebrates over towns, coasts and farmland. Mixed swift feeding flocks occur, tracking weather and prey height. Sickle wings and flight distinguish swifts from swallows but do not separate all Apus species.</p><h2>Breeding and parental care</h2><p>Building and rock cavities support nests, sometimes in colonies. Both parents contribute to incubation and feeding. Food and weather affect nestling development, while breeding timing varies among coastal sites and regions. Common Swift statistics should not be presented as measurements for this species.</p><h2>Range and Arab occurrence</h2><p>Its range is associated with the Mediterranean, North Africa and parts of the Middle East. Lebanon’s updated checklist describes local migrant breeding and passage. Seasonal strategies differ across the wider range; a summer colony does not establish winter residence.</p><h2>Seasonality and observation</h2><p>Lebanese spring passage extends from February to May and autumn passage from June to September. A November record therefore requires particular attention to identity and location. Well-exposed views of throat, face and wings help, without overstating distant photographs.</p><h2>Ecological role and conservation</h2><p>It needs safe cavities and aerial insects. Sealing occupied building openings can trap birds or prevent provisioning. Repairs outside nesting periods and retained suitable entrances help colonies coexist with maintained buildings.</p><h2>References</h2><p>Ramadan-Jaradi et al. — Updated checklist of the birds of Lebanon, Sandgrouse 42 (2020), 186–238.</p><p>British Trust for Ornithology — BirdFacts: Pallid Swift.</p><p class="photo-credit">Photo: karim Attouche — CC0 1.0.</p>

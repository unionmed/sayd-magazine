# Syrian Hunter Amani Al-Homsi: I Oppose Poaching… and I Hope Syria Passes a Hunting Law Fair to Nature and the Hunter

## Lead

Syria’s best-known woman hunter holds nature with a mother’s love, and takes her quarry with a falcon’s precision and without hesitation. It is the balance of spirit and body, of skill and knowledge — the harmony of a responsible person with the natural world.

Amani, a daughter of Aleppo, is a source of pride to her children when she is in hunting clothes, because they feel her strength and her ability; she has never let them down, not even with a meal cooked by her own skilled hands.

## Body

She said in the interview:

- What drew me into hunting was contact with hunters, my passion, and watching nature — not a love of killing anything that moves.
- The most affecting positive press comment was: “A queen from my country who managed to reconcile her household duties with her hobby, and was creative and distinctive.” Of course there are negative comments too, among them: “A woman’s place is in the kitchen.”
- I have not outshot the men except at stationary targets, and that embarrasses some of them. In the Salamiyah area, in the season for the zar‘i bird, the men always come out ahead.
- I keep my children away from hunting shotguns, for their safety.
- The obsession with showing off and drawing attention is a social blight that social media has made worse.
- Passing a new, well-ordered hunting law that respects the seasons in Syria is a basic concern for hunters there. There have been several meetings between international associations and official bodies to find a formula for a new hunting law that takes both the environment and the hunter into account. We are waiting.
- Hunting kit is essential for staying out: water, food, a knife, and an emergency kit. In dangerous country — deserts and forests — you need extras: a whistle and a flare, plus a mobile phone or a radio. Most important of all: trips into country like that should be in a group, not alone.
- On one bird-hunting trip on a riverbank in Tartous governorate I ended up fishing: I split off from my companions, went swimming, and fished, which made them laugh.
- Once I was climbing a mountain in Tartous, my foot slipped, and I rolled five metres down over stones, leaves, and thorns. I came away with bruises — but I will certainly do that trip again.
- Yes, a woman hunter among hunters helps curb poaching. I have felt that in hunting meetings and lectures I have taken part in: everyone tries to present a respectable picture of themselves, and of the difference between random shooting and responsible hunting that protects the hobby. That is a good thing.
- The only time I have hunted outside Syria was in the Qaa area of Lebanon.
- I love hunting a great deal in my own country. Syria is a hunting country par excellence, and I hope the laws come soon and let me travel and hunt in every region, as in other countries.
- My favourite quarry… thrushes and ducks.
- I call on hunters to practise ethical, humane hunting that does not harm nature and does not drive birds toward extinction.
- I hope that in our country, as in many others, the hunter will also hunt with a lens — that the relationship with nature will not be a shotgun relationship only.

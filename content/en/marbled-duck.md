<figure><img src="../../../media/publications/daily-birds-20261110/marmaronetta-angustirostris.jpg" width="2048" height="1243" alt="Marbled Duck in flight with wings extended" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style="display:block;width:100%;height:auto"><figcaption>Marbled Duck</figcaption></figure><p class="excerpt">Marmaronetta angustirostris</p><h2>Identity, life and distribution</h2><p>Marbled Duck Marmaronetta angustirostris is a small grey-brown duck with pale spotting and a dark area around the eye. The sexes are relatively similar, and a single colour in a distant photograph cannot reliably determine sex. The French name Sarcelle marbrée refers to the same species.</p><p>It depends on shallow, vegetated waters, including seasonal or semi-permanent wetlands. It takes seeds, plant material and invertebrates near the surface. Changing water levels influence site use; successful breeding requires water and cover throughout duckling development, not merely when nesting starts.</p><p>The nest may stand over water among bulrushes or on dry ground within shrubs. The 2008 international action plan gives female incubation of about 25–27 days and fledging at 50–54 days. Raising ducklings therefore requires vegetation and water to persist well beyond incubation.</p><p>Its Arab breeding range includes Morocco, Algeria and Tunisia, with breeding also documented in Libya. Iraq is important in its eastern range. In contrast, AEWA lists vagrant occurrence in Lebanon and several Gulf countries. North African breeding status cannot be extended to the whole region.</p><p>The global category is Vulnerable, VU. Wetland drainage, deteriorating water quality and disrupted water regimes threaten resources for adults and young. Maintaining a network of suitable sites through dry years matters as much as protecting one wetland in a favourable season.</p><p class="photo-credit">Photo: karim Attouche</p>

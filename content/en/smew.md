A male in distinctive plumage is white with black lines and patches; the female has a reddish-brown head and pale cheek. Young birds resemble females, so not every red-headed individual is an adult female.</p><p>It uses lakes, reservoirs and quiet waters, diving for small fish and other aquatic prey. Breeding is associated with northern forests and tree cavities near water. BTO gives a typical clutch of 7–9 eggs and female incubation of 26–28 days. The chicks hatch downy and mobile.</p><p>Surveys in Iraqi Kurdistan in 2008, published in 2009, recorded it as a winter visitor, including at Darbandikhan. It breeds in its northern forest range, while migrants use open waters farther south in winter. Freezing conditions influence movements, so observations vary between years.</p><p>The global category is Least Concern, LC. National red-listing, such as in Britain, represents an assessment at a different scale. Clean water and undisturbed resting places remain important even for a species not globally threatened.</p><p class="photo-credit">Photo: Лариса Артемьева</p>

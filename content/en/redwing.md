Red also shows beneath the wings in flight; its name does not mean that the entire wing is red.</p><p>Birds move in response to fruit and other food supplies, and winter visitor numbers can vary greatly between years. Fields, hedges and fruit-bearing trees form complementary parts of its non-breeding habitat.</p><p>It nests in northern parts of its range. BTO gives a typical clutch of four or five eggs. British breeding figures do not describe its global distribution, and winter occurrence does not establish local nesting.</p><p>Retaining fruit-bearing vegetation and quiet resting places helps visiting birds. Photographs showing the eyebrow and flanks aid separation from other thrushes.</p><p>It occurs in the non-breeding season in parts of the Maghreb, including Morocco, Algeria and Tunisia. Presence varies among sites and years.</p><p class="article-sources"><a href="https://www.bto.org/learn/about-birds/birdfacts/redwing">www.bto.org — Redwing</a> · <a href="https://www.rspb.org.uk/birds-and-wildlife/redwing">www.rspb.org.uk — Redwing</a> · <a href="https://doi.org/10.2305/IUCN.UK.2025-2.RLTS.T22708819A218591834.en">doi.org — Redwing</a> · <a href="https://avibase.bsc-eoc.org/checklist.jsp?lang=EN&amp;region=MA">avibase.bsc-eoc.org — Redwing</a></p><p class="photo-credit">Photo: Алексей Ябс</p>

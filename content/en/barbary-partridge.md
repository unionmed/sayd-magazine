<figure><img src="../../../media/publications/birds-catchup-20261104-a/alectoris-barbara.jpg" width="2048" height="1365" alt="Barbary Partridge — Alectoris barbara" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style="display:block;width:100%;height:auto"><figcaption>Barbary Partridge</figcaption></figure><p class="excerpt">Alectoris barbara</p><h2>Identity and identification</h2><p>Barbary Partridge Alectoris barbara belongs to Phasianidae and is characteristic of the Maghreb. Its grey neck is bordered by a chestnut collar spotted white; pale and dark bars mark the flanks, with reddish bill and legs. The spotted collar differs from Chukar’s more contrasting black border.</p><h2>Habitat, food and behaviour</h2><p>Rocky slopes, scrub and varied farmland provide habitat. It gathers seeds and plant material on the ground and adds insects, particularly for chicks. Groups form outside nesting. Running and concealment usually precede a short, powerful escape flight.</p><h2>Breeding and parental care</h2><p>The nest is a ground hollow screened by vegetation or a shrub. The female incubates while the male helps guard. Downy chicks leave the nest site early and feed while accompanying adults. Walking ability does not mean independence or fully developed flight.</p><h2>Range and Arab occurrence</h2><p>Its native range lies in North Africa, from Morocco through Algeria and Tunisia to Libya and parts of Egypt. GREPOM documents Moroccan occurrence and geographic variation. It is a resident Maghreb bird, not an equally widespread native partridge of the Levant and Gulf.</p><h2>Seasonality and observation</h2><p>Residents can be observed throughout the year, in family parties or nonbreeding groups. Rainfall and altitude influence breeding timing. In November, quiet rocky slopes and farmland with protective vegetation offer observation without pursuing groups.</p><h2>Ecological role and conservation</h2><p>Scrub loss, excessive hunting and releases of genetically unsuitable stock are pressures. A Moroccan genetic survey examined wild and captive populations, supporting attention to stock origins. Conserving wild populations requires habitat protection and attention to captive-stock origins; indiscriminate releases cannot compensate for degraded environments.</p><h2>References</h2><p>GREPOM/BirdLife Maroc — Perdrix gambra, Alectoris barbara.</p><p>Diversity 17 (2025), 79 — genetic study of Moroccan Barbary Partridge populations.</p><p class="photo-credit">Photo: Dario Taraborelli — CC0 1.0.</p>

<figure><img src="../../../media/publications/daily-birds-20261110/bucephala-clangula.jpg" width="2048" height="1542" alt="Female-type Common Goldeneye swimming on water" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style="display:block;width:100%;height:auto"><figcaption>Common Goldeneye</figcaption></figure><p class="excerpt">Bucephala clangula</p><h2>Identity, life and distribution</h2><p>Common Goldeneye Bucephala clangula is a diving duck with a large head and yellow eye. Males have a dark head and white facial patch; females have brown heads and grey bodies. The face pattern differs from ducks with a white band around the bill base.</p><p>It dives for aquatic invertebrates, including crustaceans and molluscs, and may take small fish. Outside breeding it uses fresh and coastal waters. It nests in tree cavities and also accepts suitable nest boxes in parts of its northern range. BTO records a typical clutch of 8–11 eggs.</p><p>The female incubates for about 29–30 days. Ducklings leave the nest cavity and move to water, but flight develops later: BTO gives a reference fledging age of 57–66 days. Old trees with cavities therefore provide a breeding resource that open water alone cannot replace.</p><p>Surveys in northern Iraq in 2008, published in 2009, documented winter occurrence, including at Darbandikhan. Breeding is associated with northern forests offering nest cavities, whereas Iraq receives birds outside the breeding season. Movements between wintering and breeding areas differ among populations and conditions.</p><p>BTO reports global Least Concern status, LC. A decline in one country’s winter visitors does not automatically represent an equal global decline. Aquatic habitats and nesting trees in the breeding range are complementary parts of conserving its annual cycle.</p><p class="photo-credit">Photo: Sunny Zhang 张迎新</p>

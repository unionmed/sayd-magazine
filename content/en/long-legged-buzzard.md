<figure><img src="../../../media/publications/birds-catchup-20261104-a/buteo-rufinus.jpg" width="1269" height="845" alt="Long-legged Buzzard — Buteo rufinus" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style="display:block;width:100%;height:auto"><figcaption>Long-legged Buzzard</figcaption></figure><p class="excerpt">Buteo rufinus</p><h2>Identity and identification</h2><p>Long-legged Buzzard Buteo rufinus belongs to Accipitridae. Broad wings, often a pale head and a frequently rufous, sparsely barred adult tail are useful clues. Colour forms overlap with Common Buzzard. Combine carpal patches, wing and tail patterns and proportions rather than relying on rufous colour.</p><h2>Habitat, food and behaviour</h2><p>Steppe, semidesert, slopes and open farmland provide hunting grounds. Rodents, reptiles, small birds and invertebrates contribute to its diet. It watches from a perch or soars before attacking. The importance of each prey group varies with habitat and availability.</p><h2>Breeding and parental care</h2><p>A twig nest stands on rocks or in a tree and may be reused. A small southwestern Iranian study documented three-egg clutches, incubation around 31 days and parental cooperation. This is a local sample, not a universal value; young require weeks of care before independence.</p><h2>Range and Arab occurrence</h2><p>Its range spans North Africa and parts of the Middle East and Asia. Lebanon’s checklist describes a breeding resident supplemented by passage and winter birds. Geographical populations vary; appearance or a single sighting cannot determine an individual’s origin or migration strategy.</p><h2>Seasonality and observation</h2><p>Lebanese residents can be seen in November alongside possible immigrants. Suitable thermal conditions permit distant observation of soaring birds. Clear wing and tail views are important, especially when several buzzard species migrate together.</p><h2>Ecological role and conservation</h2><p>Rodent predation connects it with open-country food webs. Poisons, pesticides, electrocution, persecution and habitat loss are pressures. Raptor protection requires careful rodent and poison management because contaminated prey can transfer toxins to predators.</p><h2>References</h2><p>Ramadan-Jaradi et al. — Updated checklist of the birds of Lebanon, Sandgrouse 42 (2020), 186–238.</p><p>Türk J Zool 39 (2015), 653–658 — Breeding Long-legged Buzzard Buteo rufinus in forests of southwestern Iran: feeding habits and reproductive performance.</p><p>The Peregrine Fund — Long-legged Buzzard.</p><p class="photo-credit">Photo: Лариса Артемьева — CC0 1.0. · <a href="https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/591584121">1</a> · <a rel="license" href="https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/">https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/</a></p>

<figure><img src="../../../media/publications/daily-birds-20261110/branta-bernicla.jpg" width="2048" height="1534" alt="Brent Goose standing in grass" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style="display:block;width:100%;height:auto"><figcaption>Brent Goose</figcaption></figure><p class="excerpt">Branta bernicla</p><h2>Identity, life and distribution</h2><p>Brent Goose Branta bernicla is a small dark goose with a black head and neck and short bill. Adults have a small white neck patch. Belly colour varies among populations and subspecies; colour differences alone do not establish a separate species or geographic origin from one photograph.</p><p>It uses coasts, estuaries and saltmarshes, feeding on seagrasses, algae and other plants, sometimes grazing grassland. Populations breed in the Arctic; BTO gives a typical clutch of 3–5 eggs. Migration routes and wintering areas differ among populations.</p><p>The nest is a ground depression in tundra, often near water, lined with grass and down. The female incubates for about 22–26 days. Both parents tend the young, which leave the nest within a day or two and find their own food. Audubon gives fledging at about 40–50 days.</p><p>In its report published in 2022, the Moroccan Rare Birds Committee treated it as a rare winter visitor, having previously considered it accidental. It is neither a common resident nor merely a single exceptional occurrence. In Egypt it falls within the rarities committee’s documentation remit.</p><p>Its global status is Least Concern, LC. Winter populations depend on coastal habitats vulnerable to development and shoreline change. Keeping a distance and avoiding repeated displacement protects feeding and resting opportunities.</p><p class="photo-credit">Photo: Лариса Артемьева</p>

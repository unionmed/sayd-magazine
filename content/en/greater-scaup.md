Males combine a dark head, grey back and white flanks, without the long crest of male Tufted Duck. Females are brown and often have white around the bill base, but that feature alone is insufficient for identification.</p><p>It uses shallow coastal waters and lakes, diving for food, particularly aquatic invertebrates. It breeds in northern regions. BTO gives a typical clutch of 8–11 eggs, female incubation of 26–28 days and fledging at about 40–45 days. These are reference values, not a timetable for every nest.</p><p>Its Arab-world occurrence lies at the edge of its broad northern distribution. The Moroccan checklist treats it as rare or accidental, while CMS includes Morocco and Tunisia in its range. This limited southern presence contrasts with its large northern concentrations.</p><p>Its global status is Least Concern, LC, which does not imply stability in every population. Watching from a distance rather than repeatedly flushing birds preserves feeding and resting time, especially for individuals outside their usual range.</p><p class="photo-credit">Photo: Claude Lyneis</p>

<figure><img src="../../../media/publications/birds-catchup-20261104-b/ammomanes-deserti.jpg" width="1192" height="966" alt="Desert Lark — Ammomanes deserti" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style="display:block;width:100%;height:auto"><figcaption>Desert Lark</figcaption></figure><p class="excerpt">Ammomanes deserti</p><h2>Identity and identification</h2><p>Desert Lark Ammomanes deserti belongs to Alaudidae. Sandy or brown plumage matches local rock, with a relatively strong bill and lightly marked breast. It lacks Crested Lark’s prominent crest. Geographic colour variation makes bill shape, tail and calls useful alongside upperpart colour. It is placed in the order Passeriformes. The IUCN category carried in AviList 2026 is Least Concern (LC).</p><h2>Habitat, food and behaviour</h2><p>Rocky hills, dry slopes and wadis are more characteristic than extensive bare sand. It walks for seeds and insects and uses shade in harsh conditions. Pairs or small parties may appear; movement among rocks often reveals otherwise well-camouflaged birds.</p><h2>Breeding and parental care</h2><p>A ground nest is partly sheltered by stone or vegetation and may be bordered with small pebbles. Chicks need regular food, especially invertebrates. Rainfall and food availability influence local breeding opportunities; seeing an adult pair does not demonstrate a confirmed nest.</p><h2>Range and Arab occurrence</h2><p>It is widespread in rocky dry habitats of North Africa, the Middle East and Arabia. Kuwait’s national report lists it as a breeding resident. Lebanon has a rare, local resident presence in the northeast. This contrast reflects habitat suitability rather than range size alone.</p><h2>Seasonality and observation</h2><p>Usually resident, it can be watched in suitable November habitats, with possible local movements. Clear side views of bill and tail help distinguish similar desert larks. Matching sandy colour alone is insufficient identification.</p><h2>Ecological role and conservation</h2><p>It depends on intact rocky ground and sparse vegetation. Excavation, roads and off-track vehicles can destroy nests and feeding patches. Conserving small habitat features, from shaded stones to plants, matters in a low-productivity desert.</p><h2>References</h2><p>Ramadan-Jaradi et al. — Updated checklist of the birds of Lebanon, Sandgrouse 42 (2020), 186–238.</p><p>State of Kuwait — Fifth National Report to the Convention on Biological Diversity (2014), bird list.</p><p>Royal Commission for AlUla — Birds of AlUla, Desert Lark.</p><p>AviList Core Team — AviList: The Global Avian Checklist, v2026 (20 October 2026).</p><p class="photo-credit">Photo: Tero Linjama — CC0 1.0.</p>

<figure><img src="../../../media/publications/al-ain-exhibition-20261127/pavilion-wide.jpg" width="3564" height="2376" alt="A wide view of the Emirates Falconers’ Club pavilion at the 2026 Al Ain Hunting and Equestrian Exhibition." loading="lazy" decoding="async" style="display:block;width:100%;height:auto"></figure><p class="excerpt">The second edition brings together 1,020 exhibitors and brands from 50 countries, connecting Emirati heritage with outdoor equipment and experiences.</p><p>The second Al Ain International Hunting and Equestrian Exhibition 2026 brings hunting and equestrian heritage together with outdoor equipment at ADNEC Centre Al Ain. According to Emirates News Agency (WAM), Friday, 27 November recorded the highest daily attendance of this edition so far.</p><p>The exhibition features 1,020 exhibitors and brands from 50 countries across 35,000 square metres, a 46% increase in space over its inaugural edition. Its 14 sectors cover hunting, shooting, falconry, equestrian activities, camels and salukis, alongside camping, vehicles, environmental conservation and heritage.</p><p>Visitors can learn about peregrine, saker and gyrfalcons and explore equipment used to care for and train them, including hoods, gloves, lures and tracking devices. Camping equipment, caravans and four-wheel-drive vehicles also give outdoor enthusiasts opportunities to compare products and consult specialists.</p><p>Families can explore workshops at the Young Hunters and Riders Village and displays of heritage, hospitality and Arabic coffee traditions. The Emirates Falconers’ Club pavilion adds dedicated experiences introducing visitors to falconry.</p><p>The UAE Falconers Federation also presents the Sheikh Zayed Principles of Falconry and initiatives for younger participants, highlighting falcon welfare, biodiversity and the transmission of knowledge between generations.</p><p>Organised by ADNEC Group in strategic partnership with the Emirates Falconers’ Club, the exhibition is held under the patronage of Sheikh Hazza bin Zayed Al Nahyan.</p><h2>At the Emirates Falconers’ Club pavilion: 25 years of falconry and learning for a new generation</h2><p>Among the exhibitors, the Emirates Falconers’ Club marks its 25th anniversary with a pavilion offering heritage, educational and interactive activities for families and young visitors.</p><figure><img src="../../../media/publications/al-ain-exhibition-20261127/pavilion-25-years.jpg" width="3749" height="2499" alt="The Emirates Falconers’ Club pavilion and its 25th anniversary display." loading="lazy" decoding="async" style="display:block;width:100%;height:auto"></figure><p>The Mohamed bin Zayed Falconry School bus introduces children to falconry tools and falcon anatomy. In “Fly Like A Falcon”, participants use body and arm movements to control a virtual falcon above an Emirati desert landscape while learning about its speed, vision and flying skills. An AI photo booth also creates personalised souvenir images inspired by falconry heritage.</p><p>Live falcon and Arabian saluki displays accompany traditional falconry tools and the Al Hadhira and Al Maqnas area, which explores desert life, coffee and hospitality. The “Guardians of Falconry” initiative highlights young people’s role in passing on this knowledge.</p><figure><img src="../../../media/publications/al-ain-exhibition-20261127/pavilion-entrance.jpg" width="2343" height="1545" alt="Visitors at the entrance to the Emirates Falconers’ Club pavilion." loading="lazy" decoding="async" style="display:block;width:100%;height:auto"></figure><p>A timeline, visual displays and illustrated walls trace the club’s work in falconry and environmental conservation, Abu Dhabi’s contributions, and milestones of the International Festival of Falconry. The pavilion also presents Al Sakkar magazine and its role in sharing specialist knowledge.</p><p>Activities linked to the 2026–2027 Arabian Saluki Championship include a beauty competition and simulated hunting. Club Secretary-General Majed Ali Al Mansouri described the participation as an opportunity to highlight the cultural, human and environmental value of this heritage.</p><p class="photo-credit">Sources: Emirates News Agency (WAM), the exhibition website and Sayd Magazine. Photos: Emirates Falconers’ Club.</p>

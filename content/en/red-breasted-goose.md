<figure><img src="../../../media/publications/daily-birds-20261110/branta-ruficollis.jpg" width="1620" height="1080" alt="Red-breasted Goose lowering its head to feed" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style="display:block;width:100%;height:auto"><figcaption>Red-breasted Goose</figcaption></figure><p class="excerpt">Branta ruficollis</p><h2>Identity, life and distribution</h2><p>Red-breasted Goose Branta ruficollis is a small goose patterned in black and white, with chestnut areas on the neck, breast and cheek. Distance can conceal the details and make the bird look dark; identification requires the arrangement of markings rather than an expectation of vivid red.</p><p>It feeds on plants in open country and moves between feeding grounds and waters used for roosting. It breeds in northern Eurasia and winters farther south. Conservation must connect breeding grounds, migration stopovers and wintering sites rather than protect birds at only one location.</p><p>It nests on the ground in small colonies along riverbanks, gullies and vegetated slopes. Nests may occur near raptors or gulls that deter foxes. A usual clutch contains 3–7 eggs; the female incubates alone for about 23–25 days while the male remains nearby.</p><p>Its main wintering concentrations are around the Black Sea, while Arab occurrence is limited and irregular. It is a very rare vagrant in Egypt, where sightings require records-committee documentation. AEWA includes Iraq within its native range, but the evidence available here does not establish the size of a regular wintering population there.</p><p>Its global category is Vulnerable, VU. Counts tied to places and dates, alongside protection of wetlands and feeding areas, help distinguish population change from redistribution between survey sites.</p><p class="photo-credit">Photo: Лариса Артемьева</p>

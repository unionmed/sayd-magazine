<figure><img src="../../../media/publications/birds-catchup-20261104-b/pterocles-orientalis.jpg" width="1243" height="830" alt="Black-bellied Sandgrouse — Pterocles orientalis" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style="display:block;width:100%;height:auto"><figcaption>Black-bellied Sandgrouse</figcaption></figure><p class="excerpt">Pterocles orientalis</p><h2>Identity and identification</h2><p>Black-bellied Sandgrouse Pterocles orientalis belongs to Pteroclidae. Its plump body, small head and pointed wings accompany a conspicuous black belly in both sexes. Males have simpler head and breast colours; females are more mottled. The tail is shorter and less elongated than in Pin-tailed Sandgrouse.</p><h2>Habitat, food and behaviour</h2><p>Steppe, semiarid country and sparsely vegetated open fields provide seed food collected on the ground. Birds visit water, sometimes flying considerable distances. Tolerance of dry conditions does not eliminate water requirements, especially while raising chicks.</p><h2>Breeding and parental care</h2><p>The nest is a ground scrape, usually with three eggs. Both parents alternate incubation; a west-central Moroccan study documented about 26 days. Chicks leave early and pick up food, while the male carries water in specialised belly feathers. Parental protection remains necessary after nest departure.</p><h2>Range and Arab occurrence</h2><p>Its range includes parts of North Africa, the Middle East and Asia. Moroccan studies document steppe breeding, while Lebanon’s checklist describes winter visits following increased records. Lebanese winter presence does not establish local breeding; Arab sandgrouse are not all this species.</p><h2>Seasonality and observation</h2><p>Populations include residents and birds moving with water and seasonal conditions. November observation can focus on steppe and water without obstructing feeding-to-drinking journeys. Record plumage, calls and numbers to distinguish other sandgrouse.</p><h2>Ecological role and conservation</h2><p>Steppe loss, agricultural change, hunting and disturbance around water are pressures. Ground nests are vulnerable to vehicles and trampling. Open-land mosaics and safe water support feeding and breeding; drinking sites should not become traps for capture.</p><h2>References</h2><p>Ramadan-Jaradi et al. — Updated checklist of the birds of Lebanon, Sandgrouse 42 (2020), 186–238.</p><p>Ostrich — Breeding biology of the Black-bellied Sandgrouse Pterocles orientalis in west-central Morocco.</p><p>Ferraz et al. — Animal Biotelemetry 12 (2024), 30.</p><p class="photo-credit">Photo: Лариса Артемьева — CC0 1.0.</p>

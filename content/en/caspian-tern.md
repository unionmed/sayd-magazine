<figure><img src="../../../media/publications/daily-birds-20261105/caspian-tern.jpg" width="1849" height="1494" alt="Caspian Tern in flight with a massive red bill" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style="display:block;width:100%;height:auto"><figcaption>Caspian Tern</figcaption></figure><p class="excerpt">Hydroprogne caspia</p><h2>Identity and identification</h2><p>Caspian Tern Hydroprogne caspia belongs to Laridae; its older name is Sterna caspia. The largest tern has a massive red bill, robust body and shallowly forked tail. The breeding cap is black, becoming mixed with white outside that season.</p><h2>Habitat and food</h2><p>It searches coastal water, lakes and estuaries for fish, then dives to capture them. Open shores and islands provide resting sites. Populations differ in being resident or migratory.</p><h2>Breeding and development</h2><p>The nest is a simple ground scrape. Animal Diversity Web gives one to three eggs, shared incubation of 25–28 days and fledging around 37 days after hatching. Young still depend on feeding after flight; northern breeding dates should not be transferred to the Gulf.</p><h2>Arab occurrence and seasonality</h2><p>Qatar’s current list classifies it as a common resident breeder and winter visitor, recorded in all months. November can therefore combine resident presence with winter additions. This does not make every bird a migrant or establish nesting during that month, and Qatar’s status cannot be assigned to other Arab countries.</p><h2>Observation and conservation</h2><p>Healthy fishing areas and quiet resting and nesting sites are necessary. Distant observation avoids exposing eggs and chicks when parents are disturbed.</p><p class="article-sources"><a href="https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Sterna_caspia/">University of Michigan — Animal Diversity Web: Caspian Tern</a> · <a href="https://www.bto.org/learn/about-birds/birdfacts/caspian-tern">British Trust for Ornithology — BirdFacts: Caspian Tern</a> · <a href="https://qatarbirds.org/QBList">Qatar Bird Observation Group — Qatar Bird List</a> · <a href="https://www.avilist.org/checklist/v2026/">AviList Core Team — Global Avian Checklist v2026, 20 October 2026</a></p><p class="photo-credit">Species illustration: David Bird — CC0 1.0. · <a href="https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/130867907">1</a> · <a rel="license" href="https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/">https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/</a></p>

<figure><img src="../../../media/publications/birds-catchup-20261104-c/tachymarptis-melba.jpg" width="1110" height="1110" alt="Alpine Swift — Tachymarptis melba" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style="display:block;width:100%;height:auto"><figcaption>Alpine Swift</figcaption></figure><p class="excerpt">Tachymarptis melba</p><h2>Identity and identification</h2><p>Alpine Swift Tachymarptis melba belongs to Apodidae. Larger than Common and Pallid Swifts, it shows white belly and throat separated by a dark breast band. Wings are long and powerful. The older name Apus melba refers to the same species, not an additional encyclopedia bird.</p><h2>Habitat, food and behaviour</h2><p>It catches airborne insects above mountains, coasts and towns. Feeding birds may range far from nesting cliffs, so a feeding location does not locate a nest. Groups give strong calls; the white belly helps identify high-flying individuals.</p><h2>Breeding and parental care</h2><p>Rock and building crevices hold nests made with light material bound by saliva. Three eggs are usual; both parents incubate and feed young. Species references describe roughly six to ten weeks of nest care, varying with conditions. Nest departure requires adequate flight development.</p><h2>Range and Arab occurrence</h2><p>Its range includes southern Europe, North Africa and the Middle East, extending farther south and east. In Lebanon it is a local cliff-nesting migrant breeder and passage migrant. Birds above a coast are not necessarily nesting in every building they cross.</p><h2>Seasonality and observation</h2><p>Lebanon’s checklist gives breeding from February to July, spring passage in February–April and autumn passage from July to mid-October. November follows most usual local passage. Document belly and breast band to avoid confusion with other distant large birds.</p><h2>Ecological role and conservation</h2><p>Loss of nesting crevices, disturbance and changing insect supplies are pressures. Respecting cliff colonies during climbing and construction matters. Distant viewpoints permit belly views without approaching nests.</p><h2>References</h2><p>Ramadan-Jaradi et al. — Updated checklist of the birds of Lebanon, Sandgrouse 42 (2020), 186–238.</p><p>University of Michigan Museum of Zoology — Animal Diversity Web: Tachymarptis melba.</p><p class="photo-credit">Photo: Paulo Caseirito — CC0 1.0.</p>

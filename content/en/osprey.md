<figure><img src="../../../media/publications/birds-catchup-20261104-c/pandion-haliaetus.jpg" width="1664" height="2048" alt="Osprey — Pandion haliaetus" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style="display:block;width:100%;height:auto"><figcaption>Osprey</figcaption></figure><p class="excerpt">Pandion haliaetus</p><h2>Identity and identification</h2><p>Osprey Pandion haliaetus belongs to Pandionidae. Pale underparts, dark upperparts and a dark eye stripe accompany long wings bent at the wrist. Its lighter build, facial mask and plunging fishing technique distinguish it from sea eagles.</p><h2>Habitat, food and behaviour</h2><p>It specialises in fish along coasts, lakes and rivers. Hovering or circling precedes a plunge with feet extended towards prey. Fish-carrying position and hunting behaviour aid recognition, but a raptor above the sea is not automatically an Osprey.</p><h2>Breeding and parental care</h2><p>A large stick nest stands in a tree, on rock or on a suitable platform. Three eggs are usual. References give incubation around 32–43 days and first flight roughly 48–76 days. Young continue receiving food after fledging; males provide much prey while females incubate and young grow.</p><h2>Range and Arab occurrence</h2><p>Globally widespread, it has resident and migratory populations. Coastal nesting occurs in Arabia, while Lebanon has uncommon spring and autumn passage. Not every Arab coastal sighting therefore belongs to a local breeding colony.</p><h2>Seasonality and observation</h2><p>Lebanese autumn passage extends from September to early November, with spring passage chiefly in late March and April. Early November may catch the last local migrants; resident coastal sites may hold birds year-round. Document visible rings without pursuing the bird.</p><h2>Ecological role and conservation</h2><p>Clean water, fish and safe nest sites are essential. Fishing-line entanglement, pollution, disturbance and collisions are threats. Removing abandoned fishing gear and keeping distance from nests reduce avoidable risks, especially near human activity.</p><h2>References</h2><p>Ramadan-Jaradi et al. — Updated checklist of the birds of Lebanon, Sandgrouse 42 (2020), 186–238.</p><p>University of Michigan Museum of Zoology — Animal Diversity Web: Pandion haliaetus.</p><p>Royal Commission for AlUla — Birds of AlUla, Osprey.</p><p class="photo-credit">Photo: Glenn Berry — CC0 1.0.</p>

<figure><img src="../../../media/publications/birds-catchup-20261104-a/falco-biarmicus.jpg" width="1539" height="2048" alt="Lanner Falcon — Falco biarmicus" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style="display:block;width:100%;height:auto"><figcaption>Lanner Falcon</figcaption></figure><p class="excerpt">Falco biarmicus</p><h2>Identity and identification</h2><p>Lanner Falcon Falco biarmicus belongs to Falconidae. Slender and long-tailed, adults often show a rufous crown and nape with a narrower dark moustache than Peregrine. Juveniles are more streaked. Geographic variation means separation from Saker and Peregrine requires proportions, head and tail patterns together.</p><h2>Habitat, food and behaviour</h2><p>Open rocky country, semidesert and farmland provide hunting habitat. Birds dominate the diet, supplemented by small mammals, reptiles and insects. It pursues prey in flight or attacks from a perch; pairs may cooperate. Hunting behaviour alone does not settle identification.</p><h2>Breeding and parental care</h2><p>It uses rock ledges or another bird’s old nest rather than building a substantial nest. A typical clutch contains three or four eggs; incubation lasts about 32 days. Young begin flying around 35–47 days after hatching and remain dependent on parents. Breeding calendars differ between North Africa and other regions.</p><h2>Range and Arab occurrence</h2><p>Its African and Middle Eastern range includes resident Arab populations in North Africa. Lebanon’s checklist instead describes scarce passage and winter visits without confirmed breeding. Maghreb resident status should not be transferred across the Levant.</p><h2>Seasonality and observation</h2><p>November sightings may involve eastern winter visitors or resident Maghreb birds. Distant photographs require caution with similar falcons. A sequence showing head, wings and tail helps identification without approaching nests or resting places.</p><h2>Ecological role and conservation</h2><p>Prey depletion, habitat loss, egg and chick collection and illegal trade affect wild populations. Quiet nesting sites and enforcement of protection laws matter. Reliable records should also distinguish genuinely wild birds from individuals escaped from captivity.</p><h2>References</h2><p>Ramadan-Jaradi et al. — Updated checklist of the birds of Lebanon, Sandgrouse 42 (2020), 186–238.</p><p>University of Michigan Museum of Zoology — Animal Diversity Web: Falco biarmicus.</p><p class="photo-credit">Photo: Nogga_Eugene — CC0 1.0. · <a href="https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/478301388">1</a> · <a rel="license" href="https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/">https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/</a></p>
